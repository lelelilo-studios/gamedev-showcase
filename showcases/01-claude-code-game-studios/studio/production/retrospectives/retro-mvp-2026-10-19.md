### What Went Well
- Seven stories in 43 minutes of building (22:43–23:26), each closed only after a run, a look and a kept screenshot. The rule caught real defects every time: a keeper too small to read at 390 px (001), an exhaust pipe through the tunnel (003), the crank dial under the prompt (004), a UTC date on the best score (005), six layout faults at two sizes (006), ice chips nobody could see (007).
- The brief's build order held. Bays and walking first (the risky part), screens and sound last; nothing built later forced a rebuild of anything earlier.
- The playtest was real input on both sizes, and it found the thing no story criterion could: the night had no pressure. The seed-fixed plan plus a self-playing keeper turned "too easy" into numbers (3,000 on 16 of 16 seeds) and gave the tuning a target.
- The minimal path's one-page brief was enough design: no document had to be written twice.

### What Went Poorly
- Every story passed its criteria and the game was still not a game: 7 of 7 stories closed with a night nobody could lose. The criteria tested parts; none tested whether the whole night pushes back. It took the playtest, after the last story, to show it.
- The t-shirt estimates mean nothing for this builder. Stories estimated M ("about a day") took 3 to 13 minutes; the L took 4.
- My own test tools had defects and cost time: guessed tap coordinates (006), a crank that could never hit below skill 1 in the balance keeper (008). Both looked at first like game problems.
- Twice I wrote a time into a document before checking the clock (the brainstorm log, two probe files); all were corrected against `date`, but the habit is a risk for paperwork that claims to be a record.
- `EPIC.md`'s Stories table still says Ready for all eight: at `minimal`, `/story-done` updates only the story file. The story files are the record; the table is stale by design.
